Why these lessons work this way (the science)

Each lesson follows the same evidence-based shape. Worked example first, then retrieval: studying a step-by-step walkthrough before attempting a sibling question beats attempting cold (worked-example effect — Sweller, cognitive load theory). Covert retrieval: every lesson ends with a recall-from-memory check, because practice testing was one of only two strategies rated high utility across contexts (Dunlosky et al., 2013). Spacing over cramming: distributed practice beat massed practice in nearly every one of 317 experiments (Cepeda et al., 2006) — hence the 2-day / 7-day re-test prompts. Interleaving: the three lessons mix reading and math so practice trains discrimination between problem types, not just repetition of one (Rohrer & Taylor; recent work also shows learners feel interleaving works worse even as it performs better — PNAS, 2024 — so trust the schedule, not the feeling). Dual coding: each claim is shown visually (charts) and verbally (Paivio; Mayer).
